# Day 10. Service Launch

Day 10 is not the end of development, but **the starting point of releasing the service to the world**. The service that has been completed so far from Planning → Requirements → Design → UI → Front → Back → Membership → Payment is now successfully introduced to users, prepared for operational support, and the actual **service planner's final work flow** of organizing the project is carried out with Codex.

You don't have to write a single line of code. The key to Day 10 is **“Become the best explainer of your service”**. Today's training is a process of organizing with Codex what customers might be curious about when they first encounter the service, how to help when a problem arises, and what they have learned through the project.

---

# Task 01. Preparing for service launch

## 1. Story and practice background

After a 9-day development process, the service was finally completed. Everyone on the team worked hard, and the results are great. But at this moment, the representative asks one question. *"Can we expose our service to users right now? Can users immediately understand what this service is?"*

No matter how great your service is in the world, if you don't introduce it well, no one will use it. Without a service introduction, open notice, or operating policy, users become confused and lose trust. In this exercise, we will use Codex to complete a launch materials package that users and stakeholders who are new to the service can understand at once.

---

## 2. Learning objectives

* Using AI, you can create documents that clearly and persuasively convey the core values ​​and characteristics of the service.
* Open notices can be created in different tones and formats for each channel, such as email notices and website banners.
* Draft policy documents essential for service operation, such as terms of use and personal information processing policy, can be drafted with AI.

---

## 3. Practice mission

Based on the service developed by the learner, the learner organizes basic service information in the `day10_service_overview.md` file and then requests Codex for the next task.

> "Please write a service introduction so that users who use our service for the first time can understand the core value within 5 seconds. Include the service name, three core functions, target users, and differences."

> "Please write a notice announcing the service opening in the form of an email newsletter and a banner text format at the top of the homepage."

> "Draft the terms of service and privacy policy. Write them in language that users can easily understand, rather than in legal terms."

---

## 4. Example of results

### 1) Core structure of service introduction
```markdown
# [Service Name] Introduction

## One line description
[Service name] is a [Core Value] service for [target user].

## Core features
1. **[Function 1]**: [Function Description - Focusing on user benefits]
2. **[Function 2]**: [Function Description - Focusing on user benefits]
3. **[Function 3]**: [Function Description - Focusing on user benefits]

## Why [service name]?
- [Differentiation 1 from competing services]
- [Different from competing services 2]

## Get started now
[Service URL] | [Inquiry email]
```

### 2) Service opening notice (email format)
```
Title: [Service Name] is finally open! 🎉

Hello, this is the [Service Name] team.

Today, we are finally revealing [Service Name] that we have prepared for you.

[Describe the core value of the service in 1 to 2 sentences]

Get started now → [URL]

Thank you.
[Service Name] Team Dream
```

---

## 5. Precautions and tips

* **Write in your language**: Describe in the language your users use every day, rather than technical jargon.
* **Benefit-focused**: Focus on the benefits the user gets, not the features.
* **Tips for using Codex**: After writing a draft, you can create a clearer document by asking Codex to “explain it more easily so that even elementary school students can understand it.”

---

* **Estimated lab time:** 30-40 minutes

# Task 02. Inspecting the service from the user’s perspective

## 1. Story and practice background

On the first day the service was launched, unexpected inquiries poured in. Questions like “I forgot my password, how do I find it?”, “I paid, but I didn’t receive a receipt”, and “The app won’t start.” In startups without an operations team, developers or planners must respond directly. The bigger problem is that as the same questions are asked repeatedly, team productivity drops dramatically.

Preparing FAQs and customer service guides in advance can greatly reduce these situations. Codex predicts questions users may be curious about based on service content and quickly creates a systematic response guide.

---

## 2. Learning objectives

* Difficulties that a first-time user of the service may experience can be predicted and listed from the user's perspective.
* Using AI, you can create fast, high-quality FAQ documents and customer response scenarios.
* You can learn how to derive service improvement points based on the expected list of user feedback.

---

## 3. Practice mission

Learners analyze `day10_service_overview.md` containing service information with Codex and perform the following tasks.

> "Create the 20 most common questions asked by users who are using this service for the first time in FAQ format. Divide them into five categories: membership registration, service use, payment, error resolution, and other."

> "Analyze three sections where users who are using the service for the first time tend to get stuck, and suggest problems that may occur in each section and solutions."

> “Write five scenarios in which people are likely to be dissatisfied while using the service, and write a customer response script for each scenario.”

---

## 4. Example of results

### 1) FAQ structure example
```markdown
# [Service Name] Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

## Sign up
**Q. How do I sign up?**
A. [Detailed step-by-step guidance]

**Q. I forgot my password.**
A. Click ‘Find Password’ on the login screen...

## Use of services
**Q. How do I use [Service Core Functions]?**
A. [Step-by-step description]

## Payment
**Q. How do I receive a receipt after payment?**
A. After payment is completed, send the email address you entered...
```

### 2) Example of customer response scenario
```markdown
## Scenario 1: When you cannot use the service after payment

**Situation**: Customer inquires that payment has been made but the service has not been activated

**Response Script**:
“Hello, this is [Service Name] Customer Support Team.
We apologize for any inconvenience caused.
Service will be activated within up to 10 minutes after payment confirmation.
If the problem persists even after 10 minutes..."

**Internal Processing Procedures**:
1. Check payment details
2. Check account activation status
3. Handling manual activation
```

# Task 03. Organizing project performance

## 1. Story and practice background

After launching the service, the team members gathered together. The representative says: *"You've worked really hard for 10 days. Now we need a results report to show to investors and an improvement plan for the next version."* The final task is to objectively organize the 9-day development process and clearly document what went well, what was disappointing, and how we can do better next time.

Codex receives the project outline and progress and quickly creates a draft report that can be delivered to investors and stakeholders. It also helps organize the next version roadmap based on the team's reflections.

---

## 2. Learning objectives

* You can write a result report that summarizes the entire project process and can be easily understood by stakeholders.
* You can learn the project retrospective method to analyze what went well and what needs to be improved in a balanced way.
* Using AI, you can learn how to logically organize feature priorities for the next version.

---

## 3. Practice mission

Learners write `day10_project_summary.md` that summarizes the project progress and then request the next task from Codex.

> "Write a project results report that can be delivered to investors and stakeholders based on the project progress and results. Include the background, goals, core functions, achievements, and improvement tasks sections."

> "When looking back on this project, please analyze the three best aspects and the three most disappointing aspects. Include specific reasons for each item and how it can be improved next time."

> "Create a Version 2.0 development roadmap based on the features and user feedback that were not completed in this project. Organize it using the MoSCoW (Must/Should/Could/Won't) priority methodology."

---

## 4. Example of results

### 1) Project result report structure
```markdown
# [project name] Result report

## 1. Project overview
- **Service Name**: [Service Name]
- **Development period**: 10 days (Day 01 ~ Day 10)
- **Team composition**: [Team member information]
- **Core Objective**: [One line description of objective]

## 2. Main functions and achievements
| Features | Implemented or not | Special Notes |
|------|---------|---------|
| Sign up/Login | ✅ done | - |
| Core service functions | ✅ done | - |
| Payment function | ⚠️ Partially done | Actual PG linkage incomplete |

## 3. Retrospective
### What went well (Keep)
- ...

### Points to improve (Problem)
- ...

### Try next time
- ...

## 4. Version 2.0 Roadmap
### Must Have (must improve)
- ...
### Should Have (improvement if possible)
- ...
```

### 2) MoSCoW priority summary example
```markdown
## Version 2.0 improvement task priority

### 🔴 Must Have (must be included in next version)
1. **Real PG integration**: Converts current mock payment to real payment
2. **Email authentication**: Add email authentication when signing up.

### 🟡 Should Have
3. **social login**: Kakao, Google login added
4. **Push notification**: Important event notification function

### 🟢 Could Have (if you can afford it)
5. **Dark Mode**: UI theme selection function

### ⚪ Won't Have (excluded in this version)
6. **AI Recommendation Engine**: Personalized recommendation function
```

# Mission 01. Create a service launch package

## 1. Mission Overview

All materials created so far in Task 01 to 03 are integrated into one completed launching package. Assuming that you are actually releasing the service to the world, complete the launch materials to a level that can be sent to users today.

---

## 2. Detailed tasks

1. **Complete the service introduction**: Final review of the introduction written in Task 01, and increase persuasiveness by asking Codex to “make people who read this introduction want to use the service right away.”
2. **FAQ completion**: Final review of the FAQ created in Task 02 and organize it into a format that can be uploaded to the actual service website.
3. **Complete open notice**: Complete the notice text for each channel: SNS, email, and homepage.
4. **Writing a user manual**: Create a step-by-step usage guide so that even first-time users can understand the core functions of the service.

# Mission 02. Final announcement of the project

## 1. Mission Overview

This is the final presentation session that concludes the 10-day journey. Each team presents the project, checks the presentation materials with Codex, and establishes a roadmap for the next version. After the presentation, we exchange feedback between teams and share how we use AI.

---

## 2. Detailed tasks

1. **Preparation of presentation materials**: Based on the result report prepared in Task 03, compose a slide table of contents for a 10-minute presentation with Codex.
2. **Inspection of presentation material**: Show the presentation material you created to Codex and receive feedback, “Which part of this presentation is least persuasive?”
3. **Establishment of Version 2.0 roadmap**: Based on the MoSCoW priorities created in Task 03, a three-month development roadmap for the next version will be specified together with Codex.
4. **Final presentation**: Each team will give a 10-minute presentation + 5-minute Q&A.

---

# Day 10 Key Points (Instructor Guide)

Day 10 is not **“the day to develop the service,” but “the day to successfully introduce the service to users and complete the project.”**

Students will use Codex to ask themselves questions such as:

* Can you explain the core value of the service in one sentence?
* What do users wonder about when they first encounter the service?
* When you look back on the project, what did you do best and what did you regret the most?
* Which features should be improved first in the next version?
* How can we effectively communicate the results of the project to stakeholders?

If structured in this way, Day 10 is not simply **project completion**, but is completed with training to perform the actual service planner's work with AI (Codex), from **service launch → user support preparation → performance sharing → retrospective and next planning**. In particular, the final **"Version 2.0 roadmap creation"** is a finishing task that allows trainees to experience AI not as a simple document writing tool but as **a partner in planning the next project**.
