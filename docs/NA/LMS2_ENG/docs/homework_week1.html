DAY 1 · HW-D01-02 · 50 pts

🔥 Who Owns View Counts and Ad Costs?

📌 Business scenario

VidAce's data team released a shocking report: ad impression events are being recorded 2.3 times more than real impressions, and advertisers are being overcharged at month end. Every team says, "It's not our fault."

  • 🔴 A Viewer pressed play but closed it after 3 seconds → was it counted as a view?
  • 🔴 The ad was called twice but the Viewer saw it once → what's the billing basis?
  • 🔴 A network error sent the playback signal to the server twice → who's responsible for preventing double records?
  • 🔴 The user watched 25 seconds of a 30-second ad, then closed the app → counted as completed? Partial?
🧠 Questions to think about
  • Events vs facts: are "clicking play" and "actually starting to watch" the same event? By what criteria can you separate them?
  • What ownership means: which data should be stored in which team's DB? What problems happen if two teams each store the same data?
  • Preventing double processing: when the same event comes in twice, what's the difference between "use the first" and "use the last"?
  • Billing responsibility: who should bill the Advertiser for ad costs? Should recording billing events and billing be handled in the same Domain?
💡 Example prompt (for reference — don't use it as-is)
For Domain analysis, sort the event list and scenarios yourself first, then ask AI to review.
Example prompt — HW-D01-02
[Role] You are a Domain-Driven Design expert.
[Context] On a video ad platform, playback request, actual viewing, ad impression and billing
are different events, and there's currently a double-recording problem.
[Domains to analyze] Video, Playback, Ad, Impression, Charge, User
[Request]
1) Define each Domain's Primary Responsibility and source data ownership in one line
2) Compare 2 alternative ways of splitting responsibility for "playback request → start watching → ad impression → billing"
3) Suggest a policy for handling duplicate events (e.g. Idempotency Key)
🎯 The real assignment — do it yourself
1
Event type table
Define at least 8 event types and mark each as "counted? / billed? / double-processing risk?" with Yes/No and a reason.
2
Domain responsibility map (domain_map.md)
Define at least 5 Domains and state for each ① main responsibility, ② data it owns, ③ events/data to share, ④ data it must never write directly.
3
Design a double-processing prevention policy
Compare the pros and cons of 2+ technical strategies (Idempotency Key, Redis distributed lock, etc.) to prevent double ad impression records, and describe your chosen strategy and why.
4
Decide how to handle 4 boundary scenarios
For each of 4 scenarios (leaving after 3 sec, ad called twice, network double send, closing after 25 sec), decide the handling policy and how data is recorded, with reasons.
📊 Grading (50 pts): Consistent Domain responsibility and ownership 20 pts Precise event separation 15 pts Feasible double-processing prevention 10 pts Evidence of AI judgment and self-critique 5 pts

🔥 Guarantee Playback Even If the Ad Server Stops

📌 Business scenario

At 3 p.m., a surge of VidAce users pushes ad server response time past 8 seconds. Thousands of Viewers pressed play, but are waiting with a frozen screen and no message.

  • ⚡ Ad server timeout: no response in 3 seconds — just play the video? Keep waiting?
  • ⚡ No ad candidates: inventory for that time/region is used up — how do you tell the Viewer?
  • ⚡ Leaving right before playback: the ad request finished but the Viewer closed it — bill the ad?
  • ⚡ Partial success: the ad server responded but the creative URL failed to load — play right away?
🧠 Questions to think about
  • Choosing the timeout: what if the ad server wait is 1 second? 10 seconds? Who should decide the best value, and how?
  • Fallback strategy: how much business is lost when playing without an ad? And how many users leave when the video isn't shown? Which loss is bigger?
  • User communication: in the same situation, how do "Preparing your ad" and "Your video will start shortly" feel different?
  • Billing boundary: should an ad that was requested but not shown be billed? Who should make this decision?

🔥 Design a Data Model That Separates Views, Impressions and Charges

📌 Business scenario

A serious problem came up in VidAce's month-end settlement: changing an ad campaign's budget midway also changed the previous month's billed amount. Advertiser complaints are pouring in.

Campaign(id, budget_per_day, ...)
Impression(id, campaign_id, user_id, timestamp)
Charge(id, impression_id, amount = campaign.budget_per_day / expected_impressions)

❌ Problem: Charge.amount looks up Campaign.budget_per_day live, so past charges change automatically when the budget changes

🧠 Questions to think about
  • Why normalize: why is 3NF needed? What specific anomalies happen in this scenario if 3NF is broken?
  • History vs normalization: to keep past billed amounts, you may ironically need to store some data twice. How will you resolve this trade-off?
  • Video and Impression relationship: if one video can show many ads, and one ad can show in many videos, what kind of relationship is it?
  • Aggregated vs raw data: which is more useful — a table storing total daily impressions, or one recording each impression?

[Request]
1) Define each Entity's PK, FK, main attributes and relationships
2) Explain a design that keeps past Charge history unchanged when the budget changes
3) Prove "billed amounts stay consistent before and after a campaign budget change" with 8 sample records
🎯 The real assignment — do it yourself

3NF ERD design (at least 6 Entities)

DAY 3 · HW-D03-02 · 50 pts

🔥 Resolve the Conflict Between an Ad Deletion Request and Keeping Past Charges

📌 Business scenario

An urgent request came from the legal team. Advertiser X demands their ad (confirmed as an illegal medicine ad) be deleted immediately along with all past impression records. But the finance team pushes back: the ad's past billing records are subject to tax audit and must be kept for 5 years.

  • ❌ Delete Ad record → Impression.ad_id FK violation cascades to delete Impressions
  • ❌ Delete Impression → Charge.impression_id FK violation cascades to delete Charges
  • ❌ Past billing records lost → fail tax audit, legal liability
  • ❌ Advertiser's personal data deletion request (GDPR/NDPA) conflicts with the duty to keep billing records
🧠 Questions to think about
  • Limits of Soft Delete: even with is_deleted=true, the data stays in the DB. If the advertiser exercises the right to erasure, is a simple Soft Delete enough?
  • Scope of de-identification: what info in the ad creative can you erase so it counts as "personal data deleted" while billing records stay?
  • FK constraints and archives: to stop cascading deletes, which fits better — ON DELETE SET NULL or ON DELETE RESTRICT?
  • Priority of legal requirements: when the right to erasure conflicts with local tax retention duties, which law takes priority?
💡 Example prompt (for reference — don't use it as-is)
The key is balancing technical solutions with legal requirements. Analyze the 4 conflict scenarios yourself.
Example prompt — HW-D03-02
[Role] You are a data governance expert.
[Context] When an ad deletion is requested, foreign key cascading deletes destroy past billing records.
The right to erasure (GDPR/NDPA) conflicts with tax retention duties.
[Request]
1) Design a model combining Soft Delete + personal data de-identification, with record examples before/after deletion
2) State 2 problems this model solves and 2 it doesn't
3) Evaluate a separate data archive table strategy as an alternative
[Note] Mark legal judgments as "needs review by a legal expert"
🎯 The real assignment — do it yourself
1
Detail 3 cascading delete problems
Explain step by step, from the SQL FK constraint view, how an Ad Hard Delete cascades Impression → Charge → tax report. Include the error type and business impact at each step.
2
Design a Soft Delete + de-identification model
Design the columns to add to the Ad table and how deletion requests are handled. Write a Before/After table of records before and after (de-identified), and check that billing can still be calculated after de-identification.
3
Write 4 check cases
① Charges remain after normal deletion, ② advertiser info can't be looked up after de-identification, ③ billing totals stay accurate, ④ reactivation (undoing deletion) isn't possible. Include expected input, steps and expected result for each.
4
Resolve the right-to-erasure vs tax retention conflict
Suggest 2 technically possible solutions and clearly separate what each solves and doesn't.
📊 Grading (50 pts): Meets deletion request and retention duty together 20 pts Technical completeness of the de-identification model 15 pts Specific check cases 10 pts Evidence of AI judgment and self-critique 5 pts

🔥 Critique Your Own Screen Design and Make It Better

📌 Business scenario

VidAce's UX researcher ran a usability test with 5 local traders on the campaign creation screens designed in HW-D05-01. The results were shocking.

🗣️ Participant A: "I don't know what 'under review' means. Was my ad rejected?"
🗣️ Participant B: "I got a phone call while entering the budget, and when I opened the app again I had to start over."
🗣️ Participant C: "The upload failed, but there's no explanation of what file I should upload."
🗣️ Participant D: "I pressed Submit and nothing happened. When I pressed it again it said 'This campaign was already submitted.'"
🗣️ Participant E: "I want to see how much of my ad money was spent, but I don't know how to check after making it."
🧠 Questions to think about
  • Direction of critique: which design principle does each piece of feedback break? Which of Nielsen's 10 heuristics does it relate to?
  • Tech vs UX problem: is Participant B's problem (lost progress) a UX problem or a technical one? What must change to fix it?
  • The real problem in feedback: Participant A said they didn't know the word "under review," but is the real problem the word, or the way the state is explained?
  • Checking improvements: how can you confirm the improved screen is really better? What measures should you use?
💡 Example prompt (for reference — don't use it as-is)
Critique must be specific. First read the 5 pieces of feedback and find each root cause yourself. Then talk with AI.
Example prompt — HW-D05-02
[Role] You are a UX Auditor.
[Context] 5 pieces of feedback were collected in a usability test of the campaign creation screens.
[Feedback to analyze] (paste the 5 feedback items)
[Request]
1) Classify the root cause of each feedback (UX principle broken or technical implementation problem)
2) Give an improvement for each in Before/After format
3) Write 3 usability test criteria to check the improvements
🎯 The real assignment — do it yourself
1
Root cause analysis of 5 pieces of feedback
For each, write ① the screen/component where the problem happened, ② the UX principle broken (with evidence like Nielsen's heuristics), ③ severity (1–5, by business impact), ④ whether the cause is technical or UX.
2
Before/After screen spec
For each of the 5 pieces of feedback, write the improved screen design in Before/After format. Explain how component structure, user flow and information hierarchy changed, based on UX principles.
3
3 usability recheck criteria
Write 3 measurable criteria to confirm the improved screens are really better. Each must include a measurement method (observation, survey, completion rate, etc.) and a success threshold (number).
4
Design decision record
Record separately the feedback you ① accepted, ② partly accepted (reason for not fully accepting), ③ didn't accept (reason). For ones not accepted, also write the recheck condition.
