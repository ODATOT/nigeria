Day 09. Designing a Payment Service Course Summary

A full wrap-up of UX design that prevents drop-off in the payment journey, turns failure anxiety into reassurance and maximizes trust.

📌 Part 1. Background and why this matters

Payment isn't just a technical module for receiving money; it's a psychological act of paying. Before mechanically building an external PG integration, maximize path convenience and financial trust from the 'user's' view to protect the purchase rate. In this session you use Codex Workspace to cut complex multi-step drop-off barriers, replace failure codes like over-limit and network wait with reassuring plain text, and add itemized receipts and security badges.

1. 3 frictions to overcome when designing payment services

  • Fatigue drop-off from multi-step payment paths: too many steps like delivery address, identity check and method choice make users give up.
  • Unfriendly, unclear error feedback: showing only raw codes (like `Error 1042`) without saying whether money was taken frustrates users.
  • No trust elements or visible info: unclear VAT and discount details and no refund link make users hesitate and leave right before paying.

The On-screen Trust Journey flow that stops drop-off and builds trust.

Please don't worry and try again." │ ▼ (approval succeeded) [Order confirmation page] ➔ "You can cancel/refund in one click on My Page within 7 days"

📌 Part 4. Practical tips (Best Practices)

1) Transaction Idempotency:

Not only disable clicks completely while the send flag `isPending` is true, but also send a unique UUID `orderToken` per order to the PG as an identifier, blocking double charges from rapid clicks.

2) Price Breakdowns:

Rather than one line saying 'Sign-up fee ₦50,000', breaking it down as 'Base fee ₦50,000 - launch discount ₦10,000 = final amount ₦40,000 (VAT included)' makes users much more convinced the deal is fair.

📌 Part 5. Instructor guidance (discussion topics)

  1. Easy-pay fees vs less drop-off: wallet payments like OPay or PalmPay may cost more in fees than regular cards, but cut drop-off sharply. Should an early startup prioritize saving on fees or protecting payment success?
  2. Wireless network failure during payment: if the server took the money but the user's phone screen went off and missed the notice, what recovery path best prevents support tickets?
