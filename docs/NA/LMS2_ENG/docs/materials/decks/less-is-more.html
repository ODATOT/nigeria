BEFORE WE START
Six words you will see today
Prompt
Everything you send to the AI.
“Summarise this email in 3 lines.”
System prompt
Hidden instructions the app adds before your message.
“You are a polite support agent for a bank…”
Token
A small piece of text, about ¾ of a word. AI reads and charges by tokens.
“Abuja is busy” ≈ 4 tokens
Few-shot / zero-shot
Giving solved examples in the prompt / giving none.
“happy → positive sad → negative tired → ?”
RAG
Search your documents first, then paste the best pieces into the prompt.
question → search → top 5 pages → AI
Reasoning model
A model that “thinks” step by step on its own before it answers.
OpenAI o1, DeepSeek‑R1, Claude with thinking

PART 2 · THE RESEARCH AGREES
It was not just my system
STANFORD · 2024
1 in 6 to 1 in 3
Legal AI tools from the biggest legal publishers, which already search real law documents (RAG), still gave a wrong or unsupported answer this often (17–33%).
Magesh et al., “Hallucination‑Free?”, 2024
EMNLP 2025
−14% to −85%
Accuracy fell when the input was simply made longer, even though the right answer was inside it the whole time.
Du et al., “Context Length Alone Hurts…”, 2025
Adding more documents is not a free fix. More text can mean worse answers.

PART 2 · A SECOND STORY
On hard cases, the model follows the majority
MORE “YES” CASES IN THE PROMPT
5 yes · 2 no
AI answers: Yes
MORE “NO” CASES IN THE PROMPT
2 yes · 5 no
AI answers: No
Same facts, same law, opposite answers. Similarity is not enough: the balance of opinions also steers the answer.
11
PART 2 · RESEARCH HAS A NAME FOR IT
“LLMs follow the herd”
ICML 2021 · ZHAO ET AL.
Majority label bias
GPT‑3 “is biased towards answers that are frequent in the prompt”, and repeats answers near the end (recency bias).
ICLR 2024 · XIE ET AL.
Follow the herd
When documents disagree, models “choose the side with more evidence”. Changing the order moved some models by over 30%.
Check the balance
Count yes and no cases; show both sides
Counting ≠ evidence
“Decide from the law and facts, not the number of cases”
Argue both sides
Best case for yes, for no, then the deciding fact
Flip test
Reverse the ratio or order. If it flips, it is counting

PART 3 · WHY MORE STARTED TO HURT (1 OF 2)
Long inputs blur, and rules collide
1
Lost in the middle
Like a long meeting: you remember the start and the end, not the middle.
120 cases pasted in → the key case is #63 → missed
Liu et al., 2023
2
Context rot
The longer the input, the worse the answers, even on easy tasks.
2 pages → correct 200 pages → mistakes
Chroma, 18 models, 2025
3
Rules fight
Many rules start to disagree. The model wastes effort trying to obey both.
Rule 4: “Explain your code.” Rule 31: “DO NOT add comments.”
Anthropic 2026; OpenAI 2025
13
PART 3 · WHY MORE STARTED TO HURT (2 OF 2)
Examples box it in, shouting backfires
4
Examples box it in
A smart model copies your examples too closely, even when the new case is different.
3 examples, all 1 line long → a hard case also gets 1 line
DeepSeek‑R1 paper: few-shot “consistently degrades” it (2025)
5
Shouting backfires
Capital-letter rules were for models that ignored rules. New models listen, so they overdo it.
CRITICAL: ALWAYS search User: “hello” → it searches the web
Anthropic prompting guide: write “Use this tool when…” instead
Every model has an attention budget. Each extra word spends a little of it.

PART 4 · TWO REAL EXAMPLES FROM CLAUDE CODE
Rules out, judgement in
COMMENTS IN CODE
Before: “Default to writing no comments. Never write multi-paragraph docstrings or multi-line comment blocks, one short line max…”
After: “Write code that reads like the surrounding code: match its comment density, naming, and idiom.”
TO-DO LIST TOOL
Before: about 9,100 characters of when-to-use lists and worked examples.
After: “Create and update a task list for the current session”, three status values, and one rule: only one task in progress at a time.
Anthropic: giving examples “actually constrains them to a certain exploration space”.

PART 4 · NOT JUST ANTHROPIC
Everyone found the same thing
OPENAI · 2025
Reasoning models
Give the goal, not every step. Do not write “think step by step”: they already do. Try with no examples first.
OPENAI + CURSOR · 2025
GPT‑5
“Be very thorough” made it search again and again on small tasks. Softer wording fixed it.
DEEPSEEK · 2025
R1
Examples made it worse. Just describe the problem and the answer format.
ANTHROPIC · 2025
Context engineering
Use the fewest, most useful words. A few typical examples, not a long list of special cases.
Four companies, different models, one direction: say less, but say it well.

PART 4 · WHY THIS KEEPS HAPPENING
The Bitter Lesson (Richard Sutton, 2019)
Hand-written human knowledge helps at first, then gets beaten by methods that learn and search with more computing power.
CHESS · 1997
Experts coded chess wisdom into programs. Deep Blue won mostly by searching huge numbers of moves.
GO · 2016
Decades of hand-made Go rules lost to AlphaGo, which learned from games and self-play.
PROMPTS · TODAY
Long rule lists are our hand-written knowledge. As models get smarter, the rules start to get in the way.

PART 4 · BE HONEST
Slim is not the same as safe
A RULE WRITTEN AS TEXT
Prompt: “Never delete files.”
The model usually obeys, but it can misread, forget, or find another way. Some developers reported exactly this after the cut (individual reports, not studies).
A RULE BUILT INTO THE TOOLS
Permission: delete = ask a human first
The model cannot skip it, however it reads the prompt.
Keep the prompt light. Put the rules that must never break into tools, permissions and tests.

PART 5 · SLIM, NOT EMPTY
Five things that stay
1
Goal + reason
Not “NEVER use ellipses” but “a voice engine reads this; it can’t say ellipses”
2
Your context
“Users are restaurant owners in Abuja. Prices in naira.”
3
Gotchas
“In our data, ‘closed’ means paid, not cancelled.”
4
A few examples
Two typical cases, not two hundred
5
Locks, not words
Permissions, tests, a human check
Delete: what the model can see itself, repeats, CAPITAL warnings, long lists of edge cases.

PART 5 · THE LEGAL PROMPT, BEFORE AND AFTER
Feed it vs guide it
BEFORE
You are a legal assistant. ALWAYS cite the law. NEVER guess. Here are 120 related cases […] Rule 1 […] Rule 47 […] If like case 15 but not case 82, then […]
AFTER
You help a lawyer decide which laws apply. The lawyer will check your answer, so show your reasoning and cite the article for each point. Known traps: (1) the 2021 amendment changed the time limit; (2) “employee” here excludes contractors. Two typical cases attached. If facts are unclear, list your questions instead of guessing.
Shorter, but it carries the goal, the reason, the traps and two examples.

PART 5 · TRAINER OPINION
The old ways are not dead
BIG REASONING MODEL
Guide it
Goal, reason, traps, a few examples. Hard limits in tools.
SMALL OR LOCAL MODEL
Teach it
Clear steps, several examples, a strict output format, small and careful RAG.
Data stays inside
Law, health, banks, government · NDPA 2023
Low cost
Thousands of requests a day
Offline
A laptop or phone, weak internet
Speed
Answers in under a second
OpenAI calls its reasoning models “the planners” and its cheaper GPT models “the workhorses”, which “handle explicitly defined tasks well”.

TO REMEMBER
When AI was weak, we fed it. Now it is strong, we guide it.
Goal · reason · traps · a few examples. Hard limits go in tools, tests and the human check. With a small or local model, the old tools come back.
The skill that lasts is knowing what to leave out.
