Opening question

"AI, build me an app like Jumia."
Will you get Jumia?

AI builds a product list, cart and review screen in minutes. But will that app grow?

What AI does well

  • Visible screens (UI layout, buttons, cards)
  • Common features (search, cart, login)
  • The structure of an "average" online store

What AI can't create by itself

  • Why information is shown in that order (intent)
  • What brings users back (loop)
  • Decisions refined by years of experiments (data)
You can copy the screen, but not the reason it grows. Growth hacking is the skill of finding that reason.

What is growth hacking?

Growth hacking =
fast, data-driven experiments for growth

A term first used by Sean Ellis in 2010. It crosses marketing, product, data and development to find "what creates growth" through hypotheses and experiments.

"Hacking" doesn't mean breaking in illegally — it means a creative shortcut to growth with few resources.
GoalJust one: sustainable growth
EvidenceData and user behavior, not feelings
MethodRepeat small, fast experiments
MeansChange the product itself, not just ads
Traditional marketing vs Growth hacking

Growth features hide inside the product

Traditional marketing

  • Goal: awareness, exposure, traffic
  • Scope: the top of the funnel (Acquisition)
  • Method: big-budget campaigns, results checked afterwards
  • Owner: marketing team

Growth hacking

  • Goal: the whole path — acquisition → activation → retention → referral → revenue
  • Scope: the whole user journey (AARRR)
  • Method: small fast experiments, judged by metrics
  • Owner: planning, dev, design and data together
  • Review count and rating → reduces worry about buying
  • "Up next" autoplay → keeps people watching longer
  • Verified-seller badge and feedback → trust in the other party
  • Invite-a-friend reward → referral and new users

Before growth, check:
Do people really want it?

Using growth tactics on a product people don't want is like pouring water into a bucket with a hole.

North Star Metric

The one number everyone watches:
the North Star Metric

A metric that shows both the value users get and the company's growth. Every feature and experiment exists to move this number.

Jumia (hypothesis)Orders completed after checking trust info such as ratings and official storeBrowse → trust → buy
YouTube (hypothesis)Total time users spend watching happilyDiscover → watch → next video
Jiji (hypothesis)Number of contacts (chat or call) started between buyers and sellersLocal → trust → contact
  • Sign-ups, downloads → "vanity metrics" that don't show value
  • These examples are learning hypotheses based on public info — don't present them as facts

AARRR · Pirate Metrics (Dave McClure)

Break the user journey into 5 stages

ACQUISITIONAcquisitionWhere and how do users come in?Visits · share by traffic channel
ACTIVATIONActivationDid they feel the "aha!" value on their first visit?First key action completion rate
RETENTIONRetentionDo they come back? What brings them back?Return rate · N-day retention
REFERRALReferralDo they tell others and bring them in?Shares · invite conversion rate
REVENUERevenueDoes value turn into money (or sustainability)?Purchase conversion · average order value

▼ Drop-off Where users leave between stages = the biggest growth opportunity

From funnel to growth loop

A funnel ends, but a loop keeps spinning

In successful services, user actions grow the service's value, and that value brings in new users again — a cycle.

Always write this too: the condition that stops the loop (e.g. if there are no listings in my area, nobody comes back)
YouTubeWatch → taste data builds up → better recommendations → watch longer
JijiSellers post ads → more local listings → more buyers visit → more sellers post
JumiaRate and review after buying → more trust info → new buyers worry less → more purchases and reviews
Classic growth hacks

Cases where one small feature created explosive growth

DropboxInvite a friend and both get extra storage. The reward is the product's core value itself.REFERRAL loop
AirbnbEarly on, it let hosts post listings to Craigslist too, bringing users from a place that already had crowds.ACQUISITION shortcut
HotmailA "Get your free email" link at the bottom of every email. The more people use it, the more it advertises itself.Viral loop
What they share: not ad spending, but small design choices inside the product. And AI won't think of these from a simple "build it" request.
The growth experiment cycle

Growth isn't an idea — it's repeated experiments

01 · AnalyzeFind drop-off and opportunity points in metrics and user behavior
02 · IdeasLots of "if we do X, Y will go up" hypotheses
03 · PrioritizeICE score: Impact · Confidence · Ease
04 · ExperimentSmall and fast, like A/B tests — learn from results

A good experiment hypothesis

EXP-001
Hypothesis: if we show the review count on product cards,
            more people will open the detail page
Metric: card click rate (side effect: bounce rate)
Success: click rate +10% or more

Today's key insight

  • The finished screens we see = the survivors of many experiments
  • Failed experiments and abandoned versions don't show on screen
  • AI doesn't know these experiment records and data → it gives an average answer

Why AI can’t rebuild it the same way

AI builds from only the tip of the iceberg

What's visible (what AI builds)Screen layout · buttons · cards · common features · familiar patterns
History of experimentsOrder, wording and placement that survived thousands of A/B tests
Data · network effectsValue users built up, like reviews, listings and watch history
Operations & trust policyInvisible operations like moderation, reporting, seller verification and official-store guarantees
User contextWho comes in, when, and with what worries
Business limitsDecisions shaped by fees, ads, partners, laws and regulations
Intent (Why)The design intent behind "why this feature is here"
What isn't in the prompt doesn't exist for AI. So the result becomes a "plausible average".

Retro Questions 
Answer these together in a team discussion.
1. How many growth features did AI make "on its own"?
2. Which features were missing because I didn't state them?
3. Where did AI swap in a common pattern?

5. If you did it again, what else should you observe?
6. Prove "AI is not magic" in one sentence using our results.
